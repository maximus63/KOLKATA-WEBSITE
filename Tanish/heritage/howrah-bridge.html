HOWRAH BRIDGE

Kolkata · 1943

Plate II: The massive cantilever steel superstructure of Howrah Bridge suspended above the tidal Hooghly River.

“A monumental cantilever steel lifeline uniting Kolkata and Howrah across the Hooghly.”
HISTORY

In the late nineteenth century, transportation across the Hooghly River relied on a wooden pontoon bridge engineered by Sir Bradford Leslie in 1874. As river and vehicular traffic surged following the expansion of Howrah Railway Terminus, the government appointed the New Howrah Bridge Committee in 1906 to design a permanent, high-capacity structure that would not obstruct international riverine shipping.

Following international design competitions, the consulting firm Rendel, Palmer and Tritton prepared specifications for a balanced cantilever bridge. Construction commenced in 1936 under the Cleveland Bridge & Engineering Company. Amid the intense geopolitical hazards of World War II—including Japanese aerial bombardment of Kolkata docks—the Tata Iron and Steel Company (TISCO) in Jamshedpur supplied 23,000 tonnes of a specially developed high-tensile alloy steel named Tiscrom out of the 26,500 tonnes required.

The bridge was quietly commissioned for public traffic in February 1943 without ceremonial pomp due to wartime security restrictions. In 1965, it was formally renamed Rabindra Setu in tribute to Bengal’s Nobel laureate, Rabindranath Tagore.

ARCHITECTURE

Materials: Built almost entirely of high-tensile alloy steel (Tiscrom and Tiscor) fabricated at Jamshedpur, India, ensuring exceptional structural resilience against wind shear and marine corrosion.

Architectural Influences: Designed as a balanced cantilever suspension bridge, inspired by the Forth Bridge in Scotland and Quebec Bridge in Canada, adapted to Bengal's deep alluvial silt conditions.

Structure: Spans 705 meters (2,313 feet) between the anchorages with a central suspended span of 457 meters (1,500 feet). Two immense steel towers ascend 85 meters (280 feet) above the river piers, anchored into caissons sunk over 30 meters into the riverbed.

Decorative & Technical Details: Contains no nuts or bolts in its primary structural joints; the entire superstructure was fastened together on site with heated rivets. The design completely eliminated intermediate river piers, preserving an unobstructed 29-meter maritime navigation clearance.

SIGNIFICANCE

Howrah Bridge serves as the physical, economic, and emotional gateway to Kolkata. Carrying over 100,000 vehicles and approximately 150,000 pedestrians daily, it is recognized as the busiest cantilever bridge in the world.

Beyond its transport role, the bridge has attained legendary cultural status, featured prominently in the photography, poetry, and cinema of Bengal, including masterpieces by Satyajit Ray, Mrinal Sen, and Ritwik Ghatak. It remains an enduring monument to Indian industrial engineering and resilience.

DETAILS
Location Connecting Strand Road (Kolkata) with Howrah Railway Terminus (Howrah)
Established Construction 1936–1942; commissioned February 1943
Architectural style Balanced Cantilever Suspension Steel Superstructure
Nearest Railway Howrah Junction (West) & B.B.D. Bagh / Burrabazar (East)
Opening hours Open 24 hours daily for pedestrian and vehicular traffic
Entry information Free public access (Pedestrian footpaths flank both sides)
NOTABLE FEATURES
01
Pillarless River Span:

705 meters completely clear of riverbed pylons, preventing silt disruption and allowing uninterrupted river navigation.

02
All-Riveted Construction:

Fabricated using millions of hot-driven steel rivets without structural bolts across its main joints.

03
Indian High-Tensile Steel:

Constructed with 23,000 tonnes of indigenous alloy steel provided by Tata Steel during World War II.

04
Pedestrian Thoroughfare:

Wide cantilevered walkways providing panoramic views of country boats, river ghats, and Kolkata’s historic skyline.
