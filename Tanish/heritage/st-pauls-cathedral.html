06 / COLONIAL & INDO-GOTHIC

ST. PAUL'S CATHEDRAL

Kolkata · 1847

Plate VI: The Indo-Gothic spire and serene parkland setting of St. Paul's Cathedral at the southern edge of the Maidan.

“The first Episcopal cathedral of the overseas British Empire, blending English Gothic revivalism with tropical architectural adaptations.”
HISTORY

By the 1830s, the growing European and Anglo-Indian congregation in Calcutta had outgrown St. John’s Church. Bishop Daniel Wilson spearheaded an ambitious initiative to build a cathedral worthy of being the seat of the Anglican Metropolitan of India.

Designed by Major William Nairn Forbes of the Bengal Engineers, assisted by architect C.K. Robinson, construction commenced in 1839. The cornerstone was laid on 8 October 1839, and the cathedral was formally consecrated on 8 October 1847 by Bishop Wilson.

The original towering steeple suffered severe earthquake damage in 1897 and was destroyed during the devastating Bihar-Nepal earthquake of 1934. The tower was subsequently reconstructed in 1938 based on the historic Bell Harry Tower of Canterbury Cathedral in England.

ARCHITECTURE

Materials: Dressed kiln-burnt brick finished in gleaming white chunam (polished shell-lime plaster) resembling dressed stone, with roof trusses made of iron and imported Burmese teakwood.

Architectural Influences: Designed in the Indo-Gothic style, a specialized adaptation of Victorian Gothic Revival engineered to endure Bengal’s intense solar heat and tropical monsoon.

Structure: The nave measures 247 feet long and 81 feet wide with an impressive ceiling height of 35 meters. A remarkable feature is its single wide nave constructed without interior colonnaded pillars, providing an unobstructed view of the sanctuary.

Decorative Details: The western window features a magnificent stained-glass masterpiece designed by pre-Raphaelite artist Sir Edward Burne-Jones in memory of Lord Mayo. The reredos incorporates Florentine mosaic panels depicting biblical narratives.

SIGNIFICANCE

St. Paul's Cathedral was the first cathedral built in the British Empire outside the British Isles and remains the mother church of the Diocese of Calcutta (Church of North India).

Its archives and memorial tablets document over a century and a half of administrative, military, and civic figures who shaped modern Bengal. The cathedral remains an oasis of quiet contemplation adjacent to the vibrant cultural grounds of Nandan and the Academy of Fine Arts.

NOTABLE FEATURES
01
Burne-Jones Stained Glass:

A grand rose and lancet stained glass window crafted by the pre-Raphaelite master Sir Edward Burne-Jones in 1880.

02
Canterbury Spire Tower:

A 201-foot central bell tower redesigned in 1938 based on the fourteenth-century Bell Harry Tower of Canterbury Cathedral.

03
Pillarless Nave:

An expansive hall spanning 247 feet without central support columns, featuring carved teak pews and episcopal throne.

04
Memorial Murals & Plaques:

Detailed alabaster and brass memorial tablets dedicated to civil officers, educators, and bishops of nineteenth-century India.
