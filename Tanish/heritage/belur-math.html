BELUR MATH

Kolkata · 1938

Plate IV: The Chunar sandstone central temple of Belur Math along the western bank of the Hooghly River.

“A universal architectural temple on the Hooghly harmonizing Hindu, Christian, Islamic, and Buddhist design traditions.”
HISTORY

In 1898, upon returning from his historic lectures in the West, Swami Vivekananda acquired fifty-four acres of land along the western bank of the Hooghly River at Belur to establish the permanent headquarters of the Ramakrishna Math and Ramakrishna Mission. Vivekananda envisioned not merely a monastic retreat, but an architectural symbol of universal human fraternity.

Vivekananda drew preliminary sketches incorporating motifs from global sacred monuments he had visited across America, Europe, Egypt, and Asia. Swami Vijnanananda (a civil engineer in his pre-monastic life named Prasanna Kumar Datta) translated these conceptual drawings into structural architectural plans.

The foundation stone of the grand universal temple was laid in 1935 by Swami Shivananda. Construction took three years, funded by American and Indian disciples. The temple was formally consecrated on 14 January 1938 by Swami Vijnanananda, fulfilling Vivekananda’s grand architectural vision.

ARCHITECTURE

Materials: Entirely built from buff-colored Chunar sandstone quarried from Uttar Pradesh, resting on reinforced concrete foundations.

Architectural Influences: Unites multiple sacred traditions into a single harmonious edifice: the exterior profile evokes South Indian Dravidian gopurams and Rajput palaces; the central dome echoes Mughal mosques; the ground plan conforms to Christian cathedral basilicas; and the entry pavilions recall Buddhist chaitya halls.

Structure: The main hall measures 252 feet in length and 72 feet in width, culminating in a 112-foot central tower. It features a pillarless central nave allowing thousands of worshippers an unobstructed view of the sanctum.

Decorative Details: Enshrined inside the marble sanctum is a pristine white Italian marble statue of Sri Ramakrishna seated on a hundred-petaled lotus, sculpted by Gopeswar Pal.

SIGNIFICANCE

Belur Math serves as the world headquarters of the Ramakrishna Order, directing hundreds of educational, healthcare, and humanitarian disaster-relief missions globally.

Its motto, “Atmano mokshartham jagad hitaya cha” (For one’s own liberation and for the welfare of the world), represents the nineteenth-century spiritual reform movement that reconciled ascetic contemplation with dedicated civic service.

DETAILS

Architectural style Universal Syncretic (Hindu, Buddhist, Christian, and Islamic synthesis)
Nearest Station Belur Math Railway Station / Belur Ferry Ghat (Direct river crossing to Dakshineswar)

Entry information Free public entry (Visitors are requested to observe quiet meditation inside the prayer hall)
NOTABLE FEATURES
01
The Universal Prayer Hall:

A grand nave accommodating up to 3,000 visitors, designed without interior pillars to ensure uninterrupted line of sight.

02
Swami Vivekananda Temple:

Erected on the riverside cremation spot of Swami Vivekananda, enshrining an omkar emblem in sacred quietude.

03
Holy Mother Sarada Devi Temple:

Situated directly at the riverbank, marking the final resting place of Sri Sarada Devi overlooking the water.

04
Ramakrishna Museum:

A multi-story museum displaying personal articles, manuscripts, and garments worn by Sri Ramakrishna and Vivekananda.
