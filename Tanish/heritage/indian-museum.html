INDIAN MUSEUM

Kolkata · 1814

Plate V: The classical Italianate colonnaded frontage of the Indian Museum along Jawaharlal Nehru Road.

“The oldest and largest multidisciplinary museum in the Asia-Pacific region, known affectionately to generations as Jadughar.”
HISTORY

Founded in 1814 under the guidance of Danish botanist Dr. Nathaniel Wallich at the premises of the Asiatic Society of Bengal, the Indian Museum is the ninth-oldest existing public museum in the world. Originally designated the "Asiatic Society Museum," its burgeoning collections soon outgrew its original Park Street home.

In 1867, the imperial government enacted legislation to establish a permanent imperial museum on Chowringhee Road. Architect Walter B. Granville was commissioned to design an imposing public palace, completed in 1875. The museum opened to the public in April 1878.

The institution gave birth to several of India’s foundational scientific surveys, including the Geological Survey of India, the Botanical Survey of India, the Anthropological Survey of India, and the Zoological Survey of India.

ARCHITECTURE

Materials: Monumental brickwork faced in polished lime plaster with Italian Carrara marble floors, cast-iron railings, and ornamental teak display cases.

Architectural Influences: Designed by Walter B. Granville in the Italianate Neoclassical style. The layout conforms to traditional European public palazzos, featuring grand arched cloisters and double-height exhibition galleries.

Structure: A magnificent three-story quadrangular structure built around an expansive open-air central courtyard. The building spans over 35 galleried exhibition halls supported by Doric and Ionic colonnades.

Decorative Details: Ornate vaulted archways, monumental grand stone staircases, and historic Victorian glass vitrines crafted specifically to preserve fragile organic artifacts.

SIGNIFICANCE

Popularly known in Bengali as Jadughar ("The House of Wonder"), the museum has educated generations of researchers, students, and citizens across South Asia.

Its thirty-five galleries house over 100,000 priceless artifacts across six comprehensive sections: Art, Archaeology, Anthropology, Geology, Zoology, and Economic Botany. It preserves some of the subcontinent's most revered cultural treasures, including the 2,200-year-old sandstone railings from Bharhut and an authentic Ptolemaic Egyptian mummy.

NOTABLE FEATURES
01
The Egyptian Mummy:

A 4,000-year-old embalmed Ptolemaic mummy brought to Calcutta in 1834, preserved in a dedicated climate-controlled gallery.

02
Bharhut Buddhist Stupa Gallery:

Original 2nd-century BCE red sandstone gateway and railings depicting the Jataka tales with exquisite relief carvings.

03
Ashokan Edicts & Gandharan Art:

Invaluable Maurya epigraphs and the world's preeminent collection of Greco-Buddhist stone sculptures from Gandhara.

04
Siwalik Fossil Collection:

Prehistoric fossil skeletons of prehistoric giant tortoises, mastodons, and extinct mammalian fauna from the sub-Himalayas.
