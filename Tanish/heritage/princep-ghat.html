08 / RIVERFRONT & PALLADIAN

PRINSEP GHAT

Kolkata · 1843

Plate VIII: The Greek Ionic colonnaded portico of Prinsep Ghat on Strand Road, set against the cable-stayed Vidyasagar Setu.

“A neoclassical Greek Ionic portico on the Hooghly honoring the scholar who deciphered the edicts of Emperor Ashoka.”
HISTORY

Following the untimely death in 1840 of James Prinsep—Assay Master of the Calcutta Mint and Secretary of the Asiatic Society of Bengal—the citizens of Calcutta raised a public memorial subscription. Prinsep was an extraordinary polymath whose monumental scholarly achievement was deciphering the ancient Brahmi and Kharosthi scripts, unlocking the rock edicts of Emperor Ashoka the Great and recovering ancient Indian history.

Architect W. Fitzwilliam was commissioned to design a commemorative landing monument on the Hooghly riverbank. Built in 1843, Prinsep Ghat quickly became the city’s premier ceremonial river gateway.

When the Prince of Wales (later King Edward VII) arrived in Calcutta in December 1875, he landed ceremonially at Prinsep Ghat, which was illuminated with thousands of gas lamps and lined with grand civic reception stands.

ARCHITECTURE

Materials: Monumental brickwork finished in polished white chunam plaster, resting on a raised stone terrace with broad river steps leading down to the water.

Architectural Influences: Designed in the purest Greek Ionic Revival style, inspired by the Erechtheion on the Acropolis of Athens.

Structure: The monument features an imposing open rectangular pavilion supported by fluted Ionic columns with volute capitals, carrying an entablature and triangular pediment.

Decorative Details: Inscribed marble memorial tablets detailing the scholarly contributions of James Prinsep, surrounded by manicured lawns and historic promenade lamps.

SIGNIFICANCE

Prinsep Ghat is rare among colonial monuments in celebrating epigraphy, archaeology, and historical scholarship rather than military conquests.

Today, the ghat serves as one of Kolkata’s most beloved public waterfronts. It is the principal embarkation point for traditional wooden country boat (dinghy) cruises across the Hooghly, framed by the soaring towers of Vidyasagar Setu (Second Hooghly Bridge).

DETAILS
Location Strand Road, Hooghly Riverfront, Kolkata, West Bengal 700021
Established Constructed 1843 by public citizen subscription
Architectural style Greek Ionic Neoclassical Colonnade (designed by W. Fitzwilliam)
Nearest Station Prinsep Ghat Circular Railway Station (Direct platform adjoining the lawn)
Opening hours Open 24 hours daily (Evenings especially popular for riverfront walks)
Entry information Free public entry (Wooden boat rides negotiable with local boatmen)
NOTABLE FEATURES
01
Greek Ionic Colonnaded Portico:

A pristine classical pavilion with fluted columns and architectural dentils overlooking the tidal waters of the Hooghly.

02
James Prinsep Dedication:

Historic commemorative plaques recording Prinsep's breakthrough decipherment of Emperor Ashoka's edicts.

03
Traditional Wooden Boat Journeys:

Hand-oared country boats offering sunset excursions across the river toward the Howrah shoreline.

04
Vidyasagar Setu Engineering View:

Unmatched panoramic views of India's longest cable-stayed bridge spanning 457 meters overhead.
