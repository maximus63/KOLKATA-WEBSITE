07 / COLONIAL & PALLADIAN RESIDENCE

MARBLE PALACE

Kolkata · 1835

Plate VII: The Corinthian facade and lawns of Marble Palace, situated on Muktaram Babu Street in Jorasanko.

“A nineteenth-century neoclassical palatial residence paved with ninety-six varieties of marble and filled with European masterpieces.”
HISTORY

Erected in 1835 by Raja Rajendra Mullick, an opulent merchant, landowner, and passionate art connoisseur, Marble Palace represents the pinnacle of nineteenth-century aristocratic Bengali domestic architecture. Mullick devoted his fortune to collecting fine art from auctions across Europe and Asia.

Unlike most colonial palaces that converted to state museums or fell into decay, Marble Palace remains private family property. It continues to be inhabited by the direct descendants of Raja Rajendra Mullick, who preserve the original interior decor and family temple rituals uninterrupted for nearly two centuries.

Adjoining the palace is India's first private zoo (Marble Palace Zoo), founded by Mullick in the 1830s to care for exotic birds, hornbills, and deer, which continues to function on the grounds.

ARCHITECTURE

Materials: Celebrated for incorporating ninety-six distinct varieties of marble quarried across Italy, Greece, Belgium, and India, inlaid into intricate checkerboard and medallion parquet floors.

Architectural Influences: Combines Western Neoclassical Palladian villa architecture with traditional Bengali Hindu family mansion layouts (Rajbari).

Structure: A three-story mansion dominated by a grand frontage of fluted Corinthian columns supporting a classical entablature. The ground plan features an inner quadrangle (Thakur Dalan) where traditional family pujas are celebrated.

Decorative Details: Magnificent Belgian cut-glass chandeliers, life-sized Grecian marble statues, antique grandfather clocks, and gilded French mirrors reflecting double-height ballroom galleries.

SIGNIFICANCE

Marble Palace offers an incomparable glimpse into the cultural cosmopolitanism of the nineteenth-century Bengali merchant elite (the babu class), who enthusiastically collected European Renaissance masterworks while patronizing traditional indigenous culture.

Its private art collection is globally significant, preserving authentic original oil canvases by Sir Peter Paul Rubens (including The Marriage of St. Catherine and The Martyrdom of St. Sebastian), Sir Joshua Reynolds, Bartolomé Esteban Murillo, and John Opie.

NOTABLE FEATURES
01
Rubens & Reynolds Gallery:

Authentic seventeenth- and eighteenth-century oil paintings by Flemish and British masters hanging in the original salon.

02
Belgian Cut-Glass Chandeliers:

Dozens of crystal chandeliers and enormous floor-to-ceiling gilded mirrors imported from nineteenth-century Europe.

03
Private Menagerie & Aviary:

India’s oldest private garden zoo housing peacocks, pheasants, and exotic avian species on the palace grounds.

04
Thakur Dalan Family Courtyard:

A sacred colonnaded inner courtyard dedicated to Lord Gopinathji, where classical music conferences were historically hosted.
