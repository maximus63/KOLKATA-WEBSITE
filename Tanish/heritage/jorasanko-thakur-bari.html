09 / CULTURAL & BENGAL RENAISSANCE

JORASANKO THAKUR BARI

Kolkata · 18th Century

Plate IX: The red-brick facade and quiet inner quadrangle of Jorasanko Thakur Bari in North Kolkata.

“The ancestral red-brick seat of the Tagore family, birthplace of Rabindranath Tagore and philosophical forge of the Bengal Renaissance.”
HISTORY

Constructed in the late eighteenth century by Nilmoni Tagore and expanded by Prince Dwarakanath Tagore, Jorasanko Thakur Bari is the ancestral seat of the illustrious Tagore family. It was here that Asia's first Nobel Laureate, Rabindranath Tagore, was born on 7 May 1861 and where he breathed his last on 7 August 1941.

During the nineteenth century, this mansion served as the intellectual epicenter of the Bengal Renaissance. It fostered the reformist Brahmo Samaj movement, revolutionary Bengali theatre, swadeshi enterprises, and groundbreaking experiments in Indian classical music and visual arts.

In 1961, on the centenary of Rabindranath Tagore’s birth, the mansion was acquired by the Government of West Bengal and incorporated into Rabindra Bharati University to serve as a national museum and cultural repository.

ARCHITECTURE

Materials: Deep terracotta-red country brickwork with dark green wooden louvered (venetian) shutters, lime plaster mortar, and polished teakwood floorboards.

Architectural Influences: Built in the traditional aristocratic Bengali Rajbari style, merging British colonial verandah designs with traditional North Kolkata courtyard domestic architecture.

Structure: A sprawling multi-winged complex arranged around broad open-air paved courtyards that provided ventilation during the summer months. Broad exterior balconies connect residential chambers with intellectual gathering halls.

Decorative Details: Ornate cast-iron railings, classical pediments, wide sweeping staircases, and historic gas-lamp posts preserved in their original nineteenth-century positions.

SIGNIFICANCE

Jorasanko Thakur Bari was more than a residential estate; it was an artistic laboratory where the modern Bengali language, national hymns, and philosophical universalism were forged.

Today, the museum preserves original paintings by Rabindranath, Abanindranath, and Gaganendranath Tagore, early editions of Gitanjali, and diplomatic gifts received during Tagore’s historic lecture tours across Europe, the Americas, China, and Japan.

NOTABLE FEATURES
01
Tagore’s Living Quarters:

The preserved bedroom, writing desk, easy chair, and medicine chest where Rabindranath composed poetry and spent his final days.

02
The Vichitra Gallery:

An archival gallery containing rare portraits, original manuscripts of songs, and correspondence with Mahatma Gandhi and Albert Einstein.

03
International Galleries:

Dedicated rooms showcasing Tagore’s profound cultural ties with Japan, China, the United States, and Hungary.

04
The Open Courtyard:

The central courtyard where Rabindra Jayanti (Pochishe Boishakh) is celebrated annually with music, poetry, and dance recitals.
