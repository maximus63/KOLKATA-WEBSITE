THE HERITAGE OF KOLKATA

Ten places through which the architectural, religious and cultural history of the city can be read.

⚲
1921
01 COLONIAL · INDO-SARACENIC

Victoria Memorial Hall

Queens Way, Maidan, Central Kolkata

Constructed from white Makrana marble quarried in Rajasthan, this imperial monument was designed by Sir William Emerson to serve as a national museum. Crowned by a rotating bronze Angel of Victory atop its 184-foot central dome, it houses over 28,000 historical artifacts and oil paintings within 64 acres of landscaped gardens.

1943
02 RIVERFRONT · CANTILEVER STEEL

Howrah Bridge (Rabindra Setu)

Connecting Strand Road and Howrah Railway Station

Spanning 705 meters across the tidal Hooghly without a single intermediate pillar in the riverbed, this balanced cantilever suspension bridge was engineered during World War II with 26,500 tonnes of high-tensile Indian alloy steel. It carries over 100,000 vehicles and 150,000 pedestrians daily as Kolkata’s civic gateway.

Nearest Station: Howrah Junction EXPLORE SITE→
1855
03 RELIGIOUS · BENGAL NAVARATNA

Dakshineswar Kali Temple

Dakshineswar, North 24 Parganas (Hooghly Riverfront)

Founded by the philanthropic zamindar Rani Rashmoni, this sacred riverfront complex features a majestic three-tiered nine-spired (Navaratna) sanctum dedicated to Bhavatarini, flanked by twelve identical shrines to Lord Shiva along the river ghat. The temple is venerated worldwide as the spiritual home of the mystic Sri Ramakrishna Paramahamsa.

Nearest Metro: Dakshineswar (Blue Line) EXPLORE SITE→
1938
04 RELIGIOUS · UNIVERSAL ARCHITECTURE

Belur Math Universal Temple

Belur, Howrah (Western Bank of Hooghly)

Conceived by Swami Vivekananda as the headquarters of the Ramakrishna Math and Mission, Belur Math embodies universal religious harmony through its architecture. Built from chunar sandstone, the temple seamlessly synthesizes Hindu temple towers, Christian cathedral nave proportions, Buddhist stupa canopies, and Islamic domes.

Nearest Station: Belur Math Railway / Ferry EXPLORE SITE→
1814
05 MUSEUM · NEOCLASSICAL

Indian Museum (Jadughar)

27, Jawaharlal Nehru Road, Park Street Area

Founded by Danish botanist Dr. Nathaniel Wallich under the auspices of the Asiatic Society of Bengal, the Indian Museum is the oldest and largest multidisciplinary museum in the Asia-Pacific region. Its grand Italianate colonnaded quadrangle preserves over 100,000 specimens spanning archaeology, geology, zoology, and ancient arts.

Nearest Metro: Park Street (Blue Line) EXPLORE SITE→
1847
06 COLONIAL · INDO-GOTHIC

St. Paul's Cathedral

1A, Cathedral Road, Maidan District

Designed by Major William Nairn Forbes, St. Paul's was the first Episcopal cathedral constructed in the overseas British Empire. Built in the Indo-Gothic style with a towering 201-foot central spire modelled after the Bell Harry Tower of Canterbury Cathedral, its nave contains magnificent stained glass windows by Sir Edward Burne-Jones.

Nearest Metro: Maidan & Rabindra Sadan EXPLORE SITE→
1835
07 COLONIAL · PALLADIAN RESIDENCE

Marble Palace

46, Muktaram Babu Street, Jorasanko, North Kolkata

Erected by the affluent merchant Raja Rajendra Mullick, this neoclassical mansion was constructed with ninety-six varieties of imported marble. Set within extensive private grounds that house India's oldest private menagerie, its halls contain rare European oil masterworks by Sir Peter Paul Rubens, Sir Joshua Reynolds, and Bartolomé Esteban Murillo.

Nearest Metro: MG Road (Blue Line) EXPLORE SITE→
1843
08 RIVERFRONT · GREEK IONIC PALLADIAN

Prinsep Ghat

Strand Road, Hooghly Riverfront

Constructed in honor of James Prinsep, the distinguished Anglo-Indian scholar and epigraphist who deciphered the ancient Brahmi and Kharosthi edicts of Emperor Ashoka. Designed by W. Fitzwilliam, its elegant Greek Ionic colonnade served as the ceremonial royal landing pier for visiting British royalty and foreign dignitaries.

Nearest Station: Prinsep Ghat Circular Railway EXPLORE SITE→
18th Century
09 CULTURAL · BENGAL RENAISSANCE

Jorasanko Thakur Bari

6/4, Dwarakanath Tagore Lane, Jorasanko

The ancestral seat of the illustrious Tagore dynasty, Jorasanko Thakur Bari was the birthplace and lifelong residence of Asia’s first Nobel Laureate, Rabindranath Tagore. Today integrated into Rabindra Bharati University, this brick-red courtyard mansion preserves the personal manuscripts, paintings, and living quarters of the Bengal Renaissance’s leading intellectual figures.

Nearest Metro: Girish Park (Blue Line) EXPLORE SITE→
1777
10 COLONIAL · GRECO-ROMAN TERRACOTTA

Writers' Building (Mahakaran)

B.B.D. Bagh North, Binoy Badal Dinesh Bagh

Originally built in 1777 by Thomas Lyon for the junior administrative clerks ("writers") of the British East India Company, this 150-meter-long brick edifice later received an imposing Greco-Roman French Renaissance facade of red terracotta brickwork. In 1930, it was the site of the historic armed raid by revolutionaries Benoy, Badal, and Dinesh.

Nearest Metro: B.B.D. Bagh / Lalbazar EXPLORE SITE→
